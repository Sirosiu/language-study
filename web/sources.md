# Sources and licenses

The teaching explanations combine reorganised original Language Atlas lessons with newly authored alphabet, pronunciation, word-formation, grammar and reading notes. The old historical textbook archive is not part of this rebuilt course.

The bilingual examples come from **Tatoeba contributors**, distributed through **OPUS**, under **CC BY 2.0 FR**. Each question links to a Tatoeba search for its original sentence, where its attribution and related translations can be inspected. The source folder retains the corpus URLs, releases, README and license text. The sentences are community contributions; they have not all been independently reviewed by a native-language instructor.

Citation: Jörg Tiedemann (2012). *Parallel Data, Tools and Interfaces in OPUS*. Proceedings of LREC 2012. [OPUS](https://opus.nlpl.eu/) · [Tatoeba](https://tatoeba.org/) · [CC BY 2.0 FR](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/fr/).

Vocabulary meanings, pronunciation information and available recordings derive from **English Wiktionary contributors**, extracted by **Kaikki/Wiktextract**. Text is available under **CC BY-SA 4.0** and the **GFDL**. Each entry links to its source page and revision history for attribution. Selected raw dictionary records are retained. Recording licenses are associated with their Wikimedia source files; the course streams those links rather than redistributing those human recordings.

Short teaching glosses clarify common articles, pronouns, conjunctions and other ambiguous forms. Some letter-name-only selections were replaced with authored vocabulary about studying and communication. These additions are marked in the course data; the original selected records and replacement manifest are retained for comparison. The linked dictionary supplies fuller senses and inflections.

[Wiktionary](https://en.wiktionary.org/) · [Kaikki](https://kaikki.org/) · [Wiktextract](https://github.com/tatuylonen/wiktextract) · [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) · [Wikimedia terms](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Policy:Terms_of_Use).

**wordfreq** assists vocabulary selection. These banks are useful dictionary selections, not exact lists of the 1,000 most common words. Georgian was selected using English-gloss frequency because a comparable Georgian frequency table was unavailable. Some dictionary definitions are broader or more specialised than the basic everyday sense; consult the linked entry and learn a phrase.

[wordfreq](https://github.com/rspeer/wordfreq) documents its sources and licenses. Mandarin pinyin and Japanese romaji are generated only when the selected record lacks a reading; those guides are labelled in the course. Polyphonic characters, proper names, pitch accent and contextual readings should be checked against the linked dictionary.

The offline MP3 files are **synthetic speech**, produced through Microsoft Edge speech synthesis with edge-tts. They are not recordings by a native teacher. Compare dictionary recordings and real conversation for regional speech, context-dependent pronunciation and Arabic dialect features. The voices used are named in the retained synthetic voice reference; the audio manifest records the files.

[edge-tts](https://github.com/rany2/edge-tts). The font assets are Noto fonts under the **SIL Open Font License**; packaged license notices are in assets. The CJK webfont is subset to the characters used by this course.

Grammar-form matching selects relevant examples and builds practice prompts; it is not a full syntactic parser. A form can have more than one use. In explanation questions, consult the complete sentence and reference lesson. Model translations can have valid alternatives.
